\section{Training schedule for the M1 estimator}
\label{sec:schedule}

\Cref{sec:pseudoinj} fixes the network and the loss of \cref{eq:pseudoinj-loss}. This section fixes
\emph{when} each loss term is switched on, when the graph is allowed to commit, and how a run is
monitored and stopped. The reason a schedule is needed at all: the terms conflict early in training
(the KL term pulls the posterior onto an untrained prior, the sparsity term deletes edges before the
latents mean anything, the BLAE terms need a decoder that already reconstructs), and the sink check
is irreversible. Every number below is a starting guess, to be fixed from the first runs; nothing
here has been run on real TI data or on the real IN-VAE checkpoint.

\subsection{Terms and general decisions}
\label{sec:schedule:general}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A \emph{sink check} is one call to \texttt{find\_sinks\_and\_fix}. The \emph{unresolved block}
      is the adjacency over the still-unfrozen content nodes. \emph{Binarized} means entries above
      $0.5$, which is what the forward pass sees.
\item \textbf{Start big.} Begin with $n_c$ of $4$ or $5$ or more, exceeding the budget of
      \cref{sec:reqs} if necessary, and scale down only if runtime or accuracy demands it.
      $D=n_c+n_s$ with $n_s\le2$.
\item \textbf{Validation} is a held-out slice of the training locations, never a held-out location
      and never L100. If synthetic images are included, the slice should be split by
      \texttt{src\_uuid}, so that a source image and its transported copies do not straddle the split
      (deferred; bursts are handled when the dataset is built).
\item \textbf{Graph signals are logged, never pass/fail}: edge count, number of flips of the
      binarized block, margin of the raw entries to $0.5$, frozen nodes with the epoch of freezing,
      and degrees.
\item \textbf{Dimension dependence is not linear.} Candidate edges grow as $m(m-1)/2$, KL and BILIP
      grow with $D$, while REC is a mean. Only SPARSE is normalized (below); normalizing the rest is
      deferred until runs at different $n_c$ exist.
\item \textbf{Cap policy.} A gate whose effect is reversible continues past its cap with a warning.
      The one irreversible decision, the sink check, stops the run at its cap.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The six scheduled objects}
\label{sec:schedule:objects}

\paragraph{1. Warm-up and KL ramp.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Stage A.} Train with REC and a small constant INJ ($\lambda_{\mathrm{inj}}=0.1$, a
      guess), with $\lambda_{\mathrm{kl}}=\texttt{kl\_init\_frac}\cdot\lambda_{\mathrm{kl}}^{\ast}$
      (a parameter, for example $10^{-3}$). The head must learn to reconstruct before the prior is
      allowed to pull on it.
\item \emph{Gate.} REC changes by less than $1\%$ (relative) over the last $5$ epochs.
\item \emph{KL ramp.} Linear over $10$ epochs, to the target $\lambda_{\mathrm{kl}}^{\ast}=1$, with
      $\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}=10$ (both from the toy setting of \cite{ngimpl}).
\item \emph{Cap.} $30$ epochs. At the cap: if REC is below $50\%$ of the trivial baseline (the data
      variance), continue with a warning; otherwise stop, since the head is not learning.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. SPARSE.}
Starts when the KL ramp ends, with a $10$-epoch linear ramp to a target of $0.01$ (placeholder).
The penalty is the sum of \cref{app:code:lossterms} divided by the fixed constant $n_c(n_c-1)/2$.
The logged value keeps the identity constant of $(I+A)^\top(I+A)$ (it is not subtracted), so the
logged number is never zero. No cap.

\paragraph{3. Sink check.}
The fixed \texttt{check\_epoch} clock of \cref{app:code} is replaced by a stability gate. After each
epoch, update \texttt{stable\_run}: the number of consecutive epochs in which the binarized
unresolved block did not change (reset to $0$ on any flip). When $\texttt{stable\_run}\ge W$, freeze
\emph{all} zero columns of the block at once, then reset the counters. A separate minimum dwell time
was dropped as redundant with $W$. Let $m$ be the number of unfrozen nodes; starting values:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccc@{}}
\toprule
$m$ (unfrozen) & $W$ & $\mathrm{cap}(m)$\\
\midrule
2--3 & 3 & 30\\
4--5 & 5 & 50\\
6 or more & 8 & 80\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The cap counts epochs since the last reset. At the cap the run does \emph{not} freeze anything: it
logs the flip history, the raw entries nearest $0.5$ and the nodes frozen so far, and stops. No
intervention (for example a hysteresis margin in the binarization) is added at first; whether one is
needed is decided from the first runs. Because the strictly lower-triangular design forces the
last-index node to have no children, it is always frozen at the first successful check.

\paragraph{4. MORAL.}
Target $0$ for the first runs. Later, ramp it in after the first sink check. The reference state
\texttt{prev\_adj} and \texttt{k\_min} of \texttt{model.py} are plain attributes, not buffers, and
must be saved explicitly. Frozen entries are constants and cancel in the difference, so the term
measures the drift of the unresolved block only, but it weights low-index unfrozen nodes more than
the original definition does. Reweighting this to $f$ copies of the unresolved-block drift, and
dividing by $f$, is deferred.

\paragraph{5. BILIP.}
Same REC plateau gate as for the KL ramp. Cap: $60$ epochs, then start anyway with a warning.
Linear ramp over $20$ epochs to a placeholder target of $10^{-4}$. The magnitude of BILIP is logged
once at the gate, so the target can be set against it. Deferred: a mean-over-entries normalization,
a continuous logging-only mode, and the subset fraction ($0.3$ in the code, whose docstring
attributes this to BLAE, against $10\%$ in the BLAE text; unresolved).

\paragraph{6. Run-level control.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \texttt{max\_epochs} $=250$. The worst-case budget at $n_c=5$ is about $190$ epochs.
\item When all content nodes are frozen, continue for \texttt{finetune\_epochs} (a guess of $20$)
      with the graph fixed and SPARSE and MORAL off, then stop.
\item \emph{Final report:} whether all nodes froze, the freeze order and epochs, the caps hit, the
      final graph with degrees, and validation accuracy.
\item A run is marked \emph{suspect} if any cap was hit or the graph did not finish.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Gradient-scale logging}
\label{sec:schedule:gradlog}
The weights are tuned with the Adam-based heuristic
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{scaler}=\frac{0.01}{\mathrm{LR}\times\lVert\nabla\rVert},
\label{eq:scaler}
\end{equation}
tuning each weight until its scaler is about $1$. Whether $\lVert\nabla\rVert$ is meant to include
the weight is not settled, so both versions are printed.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Computed at the target weight.} A ramping term is smaller by design, so the scaler is
      reported at the final weight, with the ramp fraction shown next to it.
\item \textbf{Per term:} ramp fraction, unweighted gradient norm, scaler at the target weight, and
      its trend. The band is loose (about $0.1$ to $10$), with different flags for ``too weak'' and
      ``too strong''.
\item \textbf{Flags only, no automatic stop}, including for blow-ups; the run is killed by hand.
\item \textbf{Computation.} Gradients are computed continuously without updating parameters, one
      backward pass per term, including terms whose weight is currently $0$. BILIP is probed less
      often because of its cost.
\item \textbf{Adjacency.} Its optimizer is SGD with momentum (\texttt{trainer.py}), where the step is
      really LR times the gradient, whereas \cref{eq:scaler} was calibrated with Adam. The adjacency
      row is therefore unvalidated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this requires of the Trainer}
\label{sec:schedule:trainer}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The sink-check gate replaces the fixed \texttt{check\_epoch} clock, and a validation pass on the
      held-out slice is added.
\item The gradient-scale logging of \cref{sec:schedule:gradlog}.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint only at epoch boundaries,} saving the non-learnable state: \texttt{stable\_run},
      the epochs since the last reset, the previous epoch's binarized block, \texttt{prev\_adj},
      \texttt{k\_min}, the REC window, the stage and ramp start epochs, all optimizer states
      (including the SGD momentum), the RNG states, and the logs. \texttt{sure\_mask} and
      \texttt{sure\_adj} are buffers and are saved with the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Open items}
\label{sec:schedule:open}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A margin in the binarization, and any intervention at the sink-check cap.
\item Normalization of KL, MORAL and BILIP across $n_c$; MORAL reweighting.
\item Splitting validation by \texttt{src\_uuid} when synthetic images are included.
\item Every starting value above is a guess; all are to be fixed from the first runs.
\end{itemize}
